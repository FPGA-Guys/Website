A common piece of feedback we get from US-based investors is some version of "great, but you're going to need to move to the Bay Area to scale." We disagree, and the reason has nothing to do with patriotism.

## Density

Quebec has one of the highest concentrations of mid-market food and beverage manufacturers in North America. Saputo, Lassonde, Olymel, Bonduelle, Cascades, Lallemand, Premier Tech — all within a 90-minute drive of our office at Concordia. That density matters because our sales motion is fundamentally physical. We need to be able to drive to a plant, walk a maintenance director through a 15-minute install on a real pump, and be back at the lab debugging by the afternoon.

## Access

A maintenance director at a Saputo facility does not return cold emails from an unknown startup. They do return introductions from the Gina Cody School's industry partnerships office, from professors who've consulted for them for a decade, from former interns who now work in their reliability teams, and from peers they met at AQFP conferences. We have those introductions. A team three time zones away does not.

## Funding architecture

Canada's non-dilutive funding stack — NRC IRAP, SR&ED, the Quebec CRIC credit (enhanced in 2025), Prompt Innovation, Mitacs Accelerate — is specifically structured to underwrite the kind of hardware-heavy, multi-year R&D that MidMon needs. Combined with a comparatively patient venture ecosystem (BDC, Investissement Québec, Inovia, iNovia), the math of building a deep-tech hardware company is materially better north of the border than it is in California right now.

## What we're not claiming

We're not claiming we'll stay Canadian forever. The Ontario manufacturing belt is the obvious Year-2 expansion, and the US Northeast — Pennsylvania, Ohio, upstate New York — is where the bulk of the addressable plant count actually sits. But starting in Quebec lets us close the first ten pilots faster, with less capital, than any other beachhead we evaluated.

It's not where we'll end up. It's where we have the unfair advantage to start.
